Notes for CS/ECE 374 A — Algorithms and Models of Computation (University of Illinois, Fall 2026), written for review: the week before an exam, or when a homework problem needs the exact definition. They follow Jeff Erickson’s textbook Models of Computation section by section, with the book’s theorem numbers. They are not a replacement for the book — read it; come here to look things up, to review, and to practice.

How this site is organized

  • Chapters (the spine, in book order) — one page per chapter with the book’s section numbers: definitions, numbered results, the exam templates, worked examples, traps, and an “On the exams” box. Each links its chapter PDF.
  • Concepts are the glossary: one short page per idea (fooling sets, the subset construction, Rice’s theorem, …) with the precise statement, how to use it, the traps, and links to every section that develops it.
  • Toolkit pages collect what is spread over the chapters: notation, every proof template on one page, the big picture of language classes, two exam reviews, how exams are graded, and errata in the book.
  • Worked problems are exam-style problems solved in full as model answers; the practice bank is where you do the work yourself — 60 problems tagged easy, medium or hard, each with a folded hint and a worked solution.

Part 1 · Models of Computation — Chapters 1–9 complete

Midterm 1 (Sep 28) covered Chapters 1–5; the final covers all nine.

  1. Strings — recursive definitions and induction on strings
  2. Regular languages — language operations, regular expressions, structural induction
  3. Finite-state machines — DFAs, the product construction, fooling sets, Myhill–Nerode, minimization
  4. Nondeterministic automata — NFAs, the subset construction, Thompson, state elimination, Kleene’s theorem, transformations
  5. Context-free languages — grammars, ambiguity, proving L(G)=L, recursive automata, Chomsky normal form
  6. Turing machines — the formal model, variants and their costs, the universal machine
  7. Undecidability — diagonalization, the halting problem, reductions, Rice’s theorem
  8. Universal models — two stacks, counters, FRACTRAN, Post’s correspondence problem, tiles, combinators
  9. Nondeterministic Turing machines — computation trees, dovetailing, P and NP, Cook–Levin

How the examples were checked

Every concrete example on the site — each regular expression, DFA, NFA, grammar, Turing machine, run trace, state count and fooling-set suffix — is checked by a script against a brute-force definition of its language (all strings up to length 10–14, and exactly by automaton equivalence wherever the language is regular); constructions such as language transformations are tested on hundreds of random DFAs. Each chapter page, concept page and worked problem was then reviewed independently against the book. The scripts are in the repository’s tools/ folder, and the slips found in the book along the way are on errata in the book.
