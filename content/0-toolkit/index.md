Reference pages, not chapters. Come here to look up a symbol, copy a proof template, see where a language sits among the classes, or review a whole half of the course the week before an exam.

- [[0-toolkit/01-notation-and-conventions|Notation and conventions]] — every symbol the site uses, how the book writes it, how to write it by hand, the other names you will meet for the same idea, and where this site departs from the book.
- [[0-toolkit/02-proof-templates|Proof templates]] — every exam boilerplate in course order (string induction, structural induction, DFA correctness, products, fooling sets, transformations, grammar proofs, diagonalization, reductions, Rice; then recursive-algorithm correctness, divide and conquer, backtracking, dynamic programming, greedy correctness, graph reductions and NP-hardness), each with its rubric line and a worked problem.
- [[0-toolkit/03-the-big-picture|The big picture]] — regular ⊂ context-free ⊂ decidable ⊂ acceptable ⊂ all languages, a zoo of famous languages in their smallest class, closure properties side by side, and how to prove that a language is or is not in each class.
- [[0-toolkit/04-review-regular-and-context-free|Review: regular and context-free languages]] — Chapters 1–5, the Midterm 1 material: what to know cold, every construction with its formula, the problem types, traps, a cheat-sheet list and a study plan.
- [[0-toolkit/05-review-turing-machines-and-undecidability|Review: Turing machines and undecidability]] — Chapters 6–9, on the final only, in the same format.
- [[0-toolkit/06-how-exams-are-graded|How exams are graded]] — dates, scope, exam policies, cheat-sheet rules, the Midterm 1 and Midterm 2 formats, and a paraphrase of every standard rubric — including the recursive-algorithm, dynamic-programming, graph-reduction and NP-hardness rubrics — with what loses points.
- [[0-toolkit/08-review-algorithms|Review: algorithms for Midterm 2]] — Algorithms Chapters 1–9 as Midterm 2 (Mon Nov 9) tests them: the official scope, the five problem types with recipes and rubrics, what to know cold per topic, which shortest-path algorithm when, the graph-reduction tricks, traps, a short-answer drill, a cheat-sheet list and a day-by-day plan.
- [[0-toolkit/07-errata-in-the-book|Errata in the books]] — the slips found in both textbooks while writing this site, each with its correction.

The exams allow handwritten cheat sheets only (one double-sided sheet for a midterm, two for the final), so these pages are meant to be *read and rewritten*, not printed: the review pages end with a list of what is worth putting on a sheet.
