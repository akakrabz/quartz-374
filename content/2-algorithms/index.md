A review of Jeff Erickson's [*Algorithms*](https://jeffe.cs.illinois.edu/teaching/algorithms/) for someone who has **already read it**: the algorithms with their recurrences and running times, the proof ideas, the patterns to reuse, the exam write-up format, and the traps. The first three chapters build one idea in three steps. **Recursion** solves a problem by trusting the recursive call on smaller instances (Ch 1), and recursion trees turn its running time into a sum. **Backtracking** is recursion over a sequence of decisions — try every choice for the next one (Ch 2) — and it is exponential because the same subproblems come back again and again. **Dynamic programming** keeps the backtracking recurrence and stores each subproblem's answer in an array (Ch 3), which turns exponential into polynomial time. **Greedy algorithms** make each decision without looking back (Ch 4) — fast, usually wrong, and correct only with an exchange-argument proof. Then the course turns to **graphs**: the vocabulary, the representations and one search that does everything — whatever-first search (Ch 5) — and its most useful instance, **depth-first search**, with its starting and finishing times, topological sort, dynamic programming on dags and strong components (Ch 6). Most graph exam problems are **reductions**: build a graph from the input and call a standard search on it.
