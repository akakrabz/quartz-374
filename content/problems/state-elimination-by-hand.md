*Problem · the regular-expression half of a Midterm-1-style DFA problem, done mechanically · uses [[1-models-of-computation/04-nondeterministic-automata#48-nfa-to-regular-expression-state-elimination|§4.8]] · concepts: [[concepts/state-elimination]], [[concepts/regular-expressions]], [[concepts/dfa]]*

> [!question] Problem
> Let $L = \{w\in\{\mathtt{0},\mathtt{1}\}^* \mid \#(\mathtt{0},w)-\#(\mathtt{1},w)\text{ is divisible by } 3\}$. For example, `0011`, `000` and `1100` are in $L$; `0` and `011` are not.
>
> (a) Give a DFA for $L$ with mnemonic state names (no proof required).
>
> (b) Turn your DFA into a regular expression for $L$ by state elimination. Show the expression automaton after every elimination.
>
> (c) Sanity-check the expression.
>
> (d) Eliminate the states in a different order. What changes?

## (a) A DFA that tracks the difference mod 3

**Idea.** Reading left to right, an algorithm only needs the difference $\#(\mathtt{0})-\#(\mathtt{1})$ of the prefix read so far, and only modulo 3: a `0` adds 1 and a `1` subtracts 1. A variable with three values is a [[concepts/dfa|DFA]] with three states.

> [!key] Answer (a)
> States $q_0, q_1, q_2$, where $q_i$ means "the prefix read so far has $\#(\mathtt{0})-\#(\mathtt{1})\equiv i \pmod 3$". Start state $q_0$; accepting states $\{q_0\}$; $\delta(q_i,\mathtt{0}) = q_{(i+1)\bmod 3}$ and $\delta(q_i,\mathtt{1}) = q_{(i-1)\bmod 3}$:
>
> | state | meaning: difference so far | $\mathtt{0}$ | $\mathtt{1}$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | → $q_0$ \* | $\equiv 0$ | $q_1$ | $q_2$ |
> | $q_1$ | $\equiv 1$ | $q_2$ | $q_0$ |
> | $q_2$ | $\equiv 2$, that is, $\equiv -1$ | $q_0$ | $q_1$ |

**Check.** `0011` visits $q_0, q_1, q_2, q_1, q_0$ and is accepted; `011` ends in $q_2$ and is rejected; the empty string stays in $q_0$ and is accepted ($0-0 = 0$).

## (b) State elimination

The tool is [[concepts/state-elimination|state elimination]] ([[1-models-of-computation/04-nondeterministic-automata#48-nfa-to-regular-expression-state-elimination|§4.8]]): give the automaton a start state $s$ with no incoming edges and a target $t$ with no outgoing edges, then remove the other states one at a time. Removing $q$ replaces, for **every** remaining pair $p, r$ — including $p = r$ —
$$
R(p\to r)\quad\text{by}\quad R(p\to r) + R(p\to q)\,R(q\to q)^*\,R(q\to r),
$$
where a missing edge is $\varnothing$. When only $s$ and $t$ are left, $R(s\to t)$ is the answer. We eliminate $q_1$, then $q_2$, then $q_0$: the state adjacent to $s$ and $t$ goes last (part (d) shows why).

**Step 0 — new start and target.** $q_0$ cannot serve as either: it has incoming edges, and it is accepting with outgoing edges. Add $s\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}q_0$ and $q_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}t$.

> [!trap] Skipping step 0
> If you eliminate $q_1$ and $q_2$ in the DFA itself, you are left with $q_0$ and a loop label $R$, and nothing tells you whether the answer is $R$, $R^*$ or $R^+$. With $s$ and $t$ in place, the last elimination produces the right star by itself. Add them whenever the start state has incoming edges or the accepting state is not a single state without outgoing edges — that is, almost always.

**Step 1 — eliminate $q_1$.** There is no loop at $q_1$, so $R(q_1\to q_1)^* = \varnothing^* = \varepsilon$. The predecessors of $q_1$ are $q_0$ (label `0`) and $q_2$ (label `1`); its successors are $q_2$ (label `0`) and $q_0$ (label `1`). That gives four pairs:

| edge $p\to r$ | old label | detour $p\to q_1\to r$ | new label |
|---|---|---|---|
| $q_0\to q_0$ | $\varnothing$ | $\mathtt{0}\cdot\mathtt{1}$ | $\mathtt{01}$ |
| $q_0\to q_2$ | $\mathtt{1}$ | $\mathtt{0}\cdot\mathtt{0}$ | $\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00}$ |
| $q_2\to q_0$ | $\mathtt{0}$ | $\mathtt{1}\cdot\mathtt{1}$ | $\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11}$ |
| $q_2\to q_2$ | $\varnothing$ | $\mathtt{1}\cdot\mathtt{0}$ | $\mathtt{10}$ |

**Step 2 — eliminate $q_2$.** Its only predecessor and only successor is $q_0$, and it now has the loop `10`, which gets starred:
$$
R(q_0\to q_0) = \mathtt{01} + (\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11}).
$$

**Step 3 — eliminate $q_0$.** Its predecessor is $s$ and its successor is $t$, both by $\varepsilon$, and its loop is the label just computed:
$$
R(s\to t) = \varepsilon\,\big(\mathtt{01}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)^*\,\varepsilon .
$$

> [!key] Answer (b)
> $$
> L = L\Big(\big(\mathtt{01}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)^*\Big)
> $$

> [!trap] Where elimination steps go wrong
> - **Forgetting $p = r$.** Two of the four new labels in step 1 are *loops*: the round trips $q_0\to q_1\to q_0$ and $q_2\to q_1\to q_2$. Drop them and the same steps produce $\big((\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)^*$, which misses `01`, `0101`, `010101`, …
> - **Dropping the old label.** $R(q_0\to q_2)$ becomes $\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00}$, not $\mathtt{00}$: the direct edge stays.
> - **Starring the wrong loop.** In step 2 the loop of the state being *removed* ($q_2$) is starred; the loop at $q_0$ gets its star only when $q_0$ itself goes, in step 3.
> - **"Simplifying" without a rule.** Rewrite only with identities you can name (Lemmas 2.1–2.2). A longer correct expression earns full credit; a shorter wrong one does not.

## (c) Sanity check

Three quick tests, each of which catches a different kind of slip.

1. **The empty string.** The expression is a star, so it matches $\varepsilon$; and $\varepsilon\in L$. ✓
2. **Everything it matches is in $L$.** The block `01` has difference $0$. In the block $(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})$, the first piece contributes $-1$ or $+2$, both $\equiv -1$; each `10` contributes $0$; the last piece contributes $+1$ or $-2$, both $\equiv +1$. So every block has difference $\equiv 0$, and so does every concatenation of blocks. ✓
3. **Short strings of $L$ are matched.** Length 2: `01` (one block `01`) and `10` $= \mathtt{1}\cdot(\mathtt{10})^0\cdot\mathtt{0}$. Length 3: only `000` $= \mathtt{00}\cdot\mathtt{0}$ and `111` $= \mathtt{1}\cdot\mathtt{11}$ are in $L$. Length 4: the strings of $L$ are the six with two 0s and two 1s, and all six match: `0011` $= \mathtt{00}\cdot\mathtt{11}$, `0101` $= \mathtt{01}\cdot\mathtt{01}$, `0110` $= \mathtt{01}\cdot\mathtt{1}\,\mathtt{0}$, `1001` $= \mathtt{1}\,\mathtt{0}\cdot\mathtt{01}$, `1010` $= \mathtt{1}\,\mathtt{0}\cdot\mathtt{1}\,\mathtt{0}$, `1100` $= \mathtt{1}\cdot\mathtt{10}\cdot\mathtt{0}$. ✓

Test 3 is what catches the forgotten loops of the trap above: $\big((\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)^*$ passes tests 1 and 2, but not `01`.

> [!intuition] Why the answer looks like this
> Each block is a walk that leaves $q_0$ and returns to it **for the first time**: either through $q_1$ (reading `01`), or into $q_2$ (by `1`, or by `00` via $q_1$), any number of round trips $q_2\to q_1\to q_2$ (each reading `10`), and back to $q_0$ (by `0`, or by `11` via $q_1$). A string is in $L$ exactly when its walk ends in $q_0$, that is, when it splits into first returns — hence the outer star.

## (d) A different order

Now eliminate $q_0$ first, then $q_1$, then $q_2$. $q_0$ is the only state joined to $s$ and $t$, so removing it hands edges from $s$ and to $t$ to *both* remaining states, and every later step must carry them along.

**Step 1 — eliminate $q_0$** (predecessors $s$, $q_1$, $q_2$; successors $q_1$, $q_2$, $t$; no loop):

| | to $q_1$ | to $q_2$ | to $t$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| from $s$ | $\mathtt{0}$ | $\mathtt{1}$ | $\varepsilon$ |
| from $q_1$ | $\mathtt{10}$ | $\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11}$ | $\mathtt{1}$ |
| from $q_2$ | $\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00}$ | $\mathtt{01}$ | $\mathtt{0}$ |

**Step 2 — eliminate $q_1$** (loop `10`): $R(s\to q_2) = \mathtt{1}+\mathtt{0}(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})$, $\ R(s\to t) = \varepsilon+\mathtt{0}(\mathtt{10})^*\mathtt{1}$, $\ R(q_2\to q_2) = \mathtt{01}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})$, $\ R(q_2\to t) = \mathtt{0}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*\mathtt{1}$.

**Step 3 — eliminate $q_2$:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
R(s\to t) = {}&\varepsilon+\mathtt{0}(\mathtt{10})^*\mathtt{1}\\
&+\big(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{0}(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)\,\big(\mathtt{01}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})\big)^*\\
&\qquad\quad\big(\mathtt{0}+(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})(\mathtt{10})^*\mathtt{1}\big).
\end{aligned}
$$

This expression is exactly equivalent to the answer of (b) — every order is correct — but it has 28 symbol occurrences instead of 10. It spells out separately the walks that end in $q_0$ without ever visiting $q_2$ ($\varepsilon + \mathtt{0}(\mathtt{10})^*\mathtt{1}$), the walk up to the first visit to $q_2$, the round trips from $q_2$, and the walk after the last visit to $q_2$. Over all six orders, the expression has 10 symbols when $q_0$ goes last, 22 when it goes second, and 28 when it goes first; the order $q_2, q_1, q_0$ gives the mirror image $\big(\mathtt{10}+(\mathtt{0}+\mathtt{11})(\mathtt{01})^*(\mathtt{1}+\mathtt{00})\big)^*$ (swap the roles of `0` and `1`).

> [!tip] Choosing an elimination order
> Eliminate first the states with the fewest edges, and keep the states joined to $s$ and $t$ for last. No order is wrong, but sizes differ, and in the worst case they grow by about a factor of 4 per elimination ($\Theta(4^n)$ in all).

> [!exam] How this would be graded
> Midterm-1-style exams ask for a DFA and a regular expression for the same language (no proof needed); state elimination is one reliable way to get the expression from your DFA when it does not come by inspection. The expression is graded **2** for syntax and **8** for correctness, with exam deductions doubled: **2** for one mistake (often a missing or extra $\varepsilon$), **4** for a few wrong strings, and **all 8** if infinitely many strings are wrong — which is what a forgotten loop or a dropped term usually costs. Hence the sanity checks. Homework answers also need a one-sentence explanation of each piece, like the intuition box above.
