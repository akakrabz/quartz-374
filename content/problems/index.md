Each problem page states the problem, solves it the way a strong student would write it on the exam — structure first, then the construction or proof, then a check — and calls out the trap at each step and how the rubric would grade it. They are original problems in the style of the course's exams; none is copied from an exam, a homework or the book. Every automaton, expression, grammar and Turing machine on them is checked by a script, and every page was re-solved from its statement by an independent reviewer.

Four of the five problem types of the Midterm 1 format — language transformations, induction, fooling set plus grammar, DFA plus regular expression — have a worked counterpart above; the fifth, true or false with a one-sentence justification, is drilled by the quick checks that open every practice page. [[0-toolkit/06-how-exams-are-graded|How exams are graded]] has the rubrics, and [[0-toolkit/02-proof-templates|proof templates]] the boilerplate to copy.
