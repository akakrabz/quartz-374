What can be computed with a given amount of memory, and what cannot be computed at all? The chapters build the answer one model at a time. **Strings** are a recursive data type, so every proof about them is an induction (Ch 1). **Regular languages** have three descriptions — regular expressions (Ch 2), DFAs, which are one-pass algorithms with constant memory (Ch 3), and NFAs, which may guess (Ch 4) — and Kleene's theorem says they describe the same languages; fooling sets prove that a language needs more than constant memory. **Context-free grammars** add recursion (Ch 5). **Turing machines** add an unbounded tape and capture everything computable (Ch 6) — and then diagonalization and reductions show that many natural questions about them have no algorithm at all (Ch 7). Chapter 8 shows how little it takes to be as powerful as a Turing machine, and Chapter 9 adds nondeterminism, which gives no more power but perhaps much more speed.

Midterm 1 (September 28) covered Chapters 1–5; the final exam (December 11) covers all nine. Every page follows the book's sections in order, links the chapter PDF, and ends with a summary and an "On the exams" box.
